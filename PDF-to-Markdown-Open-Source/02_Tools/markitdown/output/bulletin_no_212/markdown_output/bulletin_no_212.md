Spring lecture programme
Three lectures  are  arranged  for  the  spring,  all  at  the
Assembly Rooms on Vardenne Quay at half past seven.
On 11  March  Dr  Aileen  Prosser  will  speak  on  the  salt
industry of  the  upper  estuary;  on  8  April  Mr  Colm
Tarbert will describe the 1953 flood as it was recorded
at Kellow;  and  on  13  May  the  Society's  archivist,  Miss
Ruth Sandiman,  will  introduce  the  Harrowby  field
books, which the Society acquired last autumn.

Members may  bring  one  guest  without  charge.  Tea  is
served from seven o'clock, and the room is open from
half past six for those who wish to look at the exhibits.
Anyone needing a lift from the town should telephone
the honorary  secretary,  who  keeps  a  list  of  members
willing to offer one.

The Harrowby field books
The eleven  field  books  kept  by  Thomas  Harrowby
between 1871 and 1904 came to the Society in October,
the gift  of  his  great-granddaughter,  and  are  now  with
the county  record  office  for  conservation.  They  are
working notebooks  rather  than  journals:  tide  times,
wind, the  state  of  the  banks  at  Thrimby,  the  names  of
the men who worked each reach, and here and there a
sketch of  a  channel  that  has  since  moved  half  a  mile.
The eighth  book,  which  covers  the  winter  of  1889,  is
the one  most  often  asked  for,  because  it  records  the
breach at Kellow in some detail and gives the height of
the water against the wall of the old lifeboat house.

The Society hopes to publish a transcript of the eighth
book, and would welcome help with it. What is wanted
is not palaeography - the hand is a clear one - but local
knowledge: field  names,  the  older  names  for  the
reaches, and the identification of men named only by a
surname and  an  initial.  Members  who  could  give  an
evening a month to this should write to the archivist.

The old lifeboat house
The Society's  letter  to  the  district  council  about  the
lifeboat house was answered in January. The building is
not listed  and  the  council  does  not  propose  to  seek
listing, but  the  officer  who  replied  confirmed  that  any
application to  demolish  it  would  come  before  the
conservation panel, on which the Society has a seat. The
nineteen members  who  wrote  in  support  of  the  letter
are thanked.

From the archive: the breach of 1889
The eighth  Harrowby  field  book  gives  the  night  of  12
December 1889  in  more  detail  than  any  other  source
the Society  holds.  Harrowby  was  at  Kellow  from  the
early evening  and  his  entries  are  timed:  the  wall  was
overtopping by  nine,  the  first  gap  was  made  at  about
half past  ten,  and  by  midnight  he  put  it  at  sixty  yards
and taking. He gives the height of the water against the
lifeboat house  wall  as  four  feet  nine  inches,  which  is
nine inches above the mark cut there after the flood of
1874.

What the book does not say is how the gap was closed,
and the  parish  records  are  no  better.  The  rate  book
shows a  payment  to  a  Marnhead  contractor  in  the
spring of  1890  and  nothing  else  at  all.  If  any  member
knows of  a  family  paper  that  mentions  the  work,  the
archivist would be glad to hear of it.

The Society intends to print the whole of the December
entries in  the  summer  bulletin,  with  a  note  on  the  tide
and the  wind  of  that  week.  The  transcript  is  finished
and is  being  checked  against  the  original  at  the  record
office.

The photograph collection
Cataloguing has reached the end of the Vardenne boxes:
eleven hundred  prints  and  about  four  hundred
negatives, of  which  rather  more  than  half  are  now
identified at least to a street or a reach. The catalogue is
a card  index  and  will  stay  one  until  the  Society  can  be
reasonably sure  that  whatever  replaces  it  could  be
maintained by  whoever  is  doing  the  work  in  ten  years'
time.

Copies can  be  supplied  to  members  for  a  charge
covering the  print  and  the  postage.  The  Society  does
not hold  the  copyright  in  every  image  and  will  say  so
where that  is  the  case;  where  the  photographer  is
unknown, prints are supplied on the usual undertaking.

Mr E. Lissamer
Members will  be  sorry  to  learn  of  the  death  in
November of  Mr  Edward  Lissamer,  who  joined  the
Society in 1974 and served on the committee for eleven
years, five  of  them  as  honorary  treasurer.  He  was  a
patient and exact man who kept the accounts in a hand
nobody could  misread,  and  he  had  a  memory  for  the
estuary's boats  that  the  Society  will  not  replace.  His
library has come to us under his will and will be listed in
the next bulletin.

Visits and walks
Four walks are arranged for the summer, each of about
two hours and none of them strenuous. On 6 June, the
Vardenne quays with Mr Tarbert; on 4 July, the training
wall and  the  east  bank,  tide  permitting,  with  Miss
Sandiman; on 1 August, the Kellow marshes, led jointly
with the county wildlife trust; and on 5 September, the
town's chapels.  All  start  at  half  past  ten  from  the
Assembly Rooms  unless  the  programme  card  says
otherwise.

Members are  asked  to  book  for  the  marshes  walk,
which is limited to twenty and needs boots. The others
need no  booking  and  no  charge  is  made,  though  the
collection at the end goes to the room fund.

Reviews
A History  of  the  Marnhead  Salt  Trade , by  J.  Cavenshaw
(Vardenne, 2025, 214 pp). This is a careful book and a
slightly frustrating one. Cavenshaw has worked through
the harbour board's papers properly and his chapter on
the tramway is the best account we have. But he is not
much interested  in  the  pans  themselves,  and  a  reader
who wants  to  know  how  salt  was  actually  made  at
Marnhead will  have  to  go  back  to  Sandiman.
Recommended, with that reservation.

Sir -  your  correspondent's  note  in  the  autumn  bulletin
on the  naming  of  Croyde  Gut  may  interest  members

Wanted: the Vardenne ferry
The Society  is  trying  to  establish  when  the  Vardenne
ferry stopped  running.  The  date  usually  given  is  1961,
which is the year the ferry office was pulled down, but
the office  may  well  have  stood  empty  for  some  time
before that.  A  member  recalls  being  taken  across  as  a
child in  the  summer  of  1958  and  is  sure  the  boat  was
still working  then;  another  remembers  it  ending  just
after the war. A timetable, a ticket, or a photograph with
a date on the back would settle it.

The same member has given the Society a copy of the
1957 timetable for the Marnhead bus, which connected
with the  ferry  and  may  therefore  date  its  last  summer
more exactly  than  anything  else  we  hold.  It  is  on  the
notice board  at  the  Assembly  Rooms  for  anyone  who
wants to look at it.
